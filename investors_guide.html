Investor Guide

The Robot Services Exchange

The RSE is an open marketplace for robot labor. Buyers post work; providers (robots and their operators) claim matches by capability, location, reputation, and price. Both sides rate delivery. Liquidity compounds the way it does on any two-sided market: more density, better matches, higher trust, lower friction.

We are not building another closed fleet or vertical SaaS. We are building the neutral exchange layer — the match loop, the job channel, ratings, seats, and the primitives that let anyone request or deploy physical work. Value accrues to the platform as volume and density grow.

The opportunity

Physical work is moving into a liquid market. The same flywheel that created multi-hundred-billion-dollar digital marketplaces is now available for robot labor: open posting, competitive grabbing, portable reputation, and API-first participation by both humans and machines.

What we sell to capital

Long-duration ownership in the exchange layer, plus preferred commercial rights that strengthen only as the network metrics improve. Capital is structured to stay; rights are structured to deepen with success.

What we sell to talent

Founding seats from the primary issuance pool, 10-year equity vesting, ordinary non-competes, and the same permanent-partner mindset we offer investors. Capital and team arrive together.

Why permanent capital

Ten-year fund clocks force artificial exits. Platforms that compound over decades need capital that can stay. We therefore structure investor commitments as permanent or very long-dated vehicles (10–15+ years) with preferred commercial rights rather than hard territorial monopolies. Preferential routing, data access, referral economics, or category preference expand only when measurable network metrics improve — GMV, density, seats issued, jobs completed. Both sides have incentive to grow the pie.

Direction in one sentence. Patient capital + preferred rights that ratchet with network performance + founding-partner hiring rights so the raise also builds the team.

Permanent capital structure

Capital side

  • Permanent / evergreen or 15-year+ closed-end vehicle
  • Capital called over time against milestones or as needed
  • Uncalled commitments remain live — no artificial distribution clock
  • Equity (or equity-like) ownership plus preferred commercial rights

Preferred rights (the exclusivity layer)

  • Priority matching / routing in defined categories or geographies
  • Preferential data access and referral economics
  • First-look on hardware or franchise opportunities
  • Rights strengthen only with network metrics — variable deepening

What “variable deepening” means in practice

Preferential rights are not absolute territorial monopolies. They are preferential and can expand or improve only as measurable density, GMV, seat issuance, or jobs completed cross agreed thresholds. Underperformance does not expand rights. Both the exchange and the partner therefore share the incentive to compound the network rather than protect a static franchise.

The RSE Partnership mechanics & team

Founding Partner seats

Meaningful capital partners may also take a Founding Partner seat. That seat carries preferred commercial rights and talent-side rights:

  • First-refusal or preferred rights on key hires in their area of focus (or overall)
  • Advisory board or observer rights
  • Ability to co-sponsor or introduce talent

This turns the raise into a dual attractor: capital and high-caliber people arrive together. The same permanent-partner language appears on the hiring page so candidates see the long horizon from day one.

Team equity & alignment

10-year vesting

Key employees: 1-year cliff, then monthly (or quarterly) over the remaining nine years. Performance acceleration available for exceptional contribution. Long vesting does the heavy lifting on retention.

Ordinary non-competes

1–2 years post-employment, limited to direct competitive robot-labor exchanges or the specific functional domain held. Reasonable geographic and activity carve-outs. Not onerous; designed to be enforceable and fair.

Founding seats & Golden Seats

Why this structure has worked historically

Permanent / long-dated capital vehicles (Sequoia’s shift away from rigid 10-year clocks; Berkshire-style multi-decade ownership) remove pressure to force exits and let platforms compound. Preferred commercial relationships that improve with performance create alignment without the toxicity of absolute exclusivity. Bundling capital with hiring rights turns the syndicate into a talent network. The combination has produced durable partnerships across technology platforms and long-horizon ownership models.
