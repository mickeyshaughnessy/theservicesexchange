Business Plan

The Robot Services Exchange — Open marketplace for robot labor

1. Executive summary

The RSE is the neutral exchange layer for robot labor: buyers post work in plain language; providers (robots and operators) grab matches by capability, location, reputation, and price; both sides rate delivery. The platform captures value as density grows through take-rate on cleared jobs, eternal supply seats, hardware referrals, attention products, franchises, fund platform fees, an insurance/SLA path for institutional volume, and design services.

We raise permanent / long-dated capital (10–15+ years) and grant preferred commercial rights that deepen only as network metrics improve. Seat and equity purchases participate in the Hyperion Fund with those same dollars: the Fund buys remnant real estate and robot-advantaged SMBs (and holds liquid markets), charges an annual management fee, and allows only earned profits — not the initial purchase price — to transfer out. Founding Partner seats also carry preferred hiring rights so capital and talent arrive together. Key team equity uses a 10-year vesting schedule and ordinary non-competes. The structure is designed for a multi-decade compounding marketplace, not a forced 10-year exit clock.

The ask (orientation)

Permanent or long-dated capital commitments under conservative / base / aggressive scenarios modeled on the investors page ($0.5T / $1.5T / $2.0T raise dials for planning). Preferred rights + optional Founding Partner hiring rights. Not a public offering.

Use of capital (high level)

Exchange reliability & escrow, density, seat ops, franchise support, and team. Seat and equity proceeds also participate in the Hyperion Fund, which deploys into income-producing remnant real estate and robot-advantaged SMBs (first wave: ~12 STRs and ~4 health-aide dispatch books), plus liquid markets.

2. Problem

Robot labor is arriving in the physical world, but the market for it is still fragmented: closed fleets, one-off integrations, opaque pricing, no portable reputation, and no neutral place where any buyer can post work and any capable provider can compete. Buyers cannot discover reliable capacity at transparent prices. Operators and fleets cannot efficiently fill underutilized robots. Hardware makers lack a liquid demand surface. The result is slower adoption, higher friction, and value captured by vertical silos instead of an open market.

3. Solution

An open, API-first exchange. Demand is people bidding for a service on the website (job, price, location — one-shot or recurring). Supply is robots grabbing the fit on Grab Job or via the API at rse-api.com. Both sides sign and rate via /sign_job. Seats (ERC-721 on Base when verification is on) gate robot grab access. Reputation is mutual and portable. Settlement stays between the parties while the platform records price. The website is the human marketplace; the API is how machines integrate.

4. Market opportunity

Physical work that can be specified, matched, and rated is the addressable surface. As robot capability and density rise, the same network effects that scaled digital labor and asset marketplaces apply: more supply improves match quality for demand; more demand improves utilization for supply; reputation and history raise trust and reduce friction. The investors-page base scenario orients around ~$30T robot-labor GMV in 2040 (~half of ~$60T 2025 human labor GMV) with a ~5% take-rate, plus eight streams. Seat primary issuance is the bulk of modeled project revenue (base peak near $50T in 2035 at $100k × 500M new seats). Actual outcomes depend on density, regulatory environment, and execution.

7. Go-to-market & density strategy

  • API-first so fleets, operators, and agents integrate natively
  • Seat issuance to gate and monetize supply access
  • Design partners with fleet capacity or recurring facility demand (campus, logistics, inspection, security)
  • Franchise rollout (Garage, Lighthouse) for physical density nodes
  • Campaigns for bulk demand; job parties for multi-agent work
  • Hardware catalog + financing referrals to lower buyer friction
  • Preferred commercial rights for capital partners who also accelerate supply or demand
  • Design services that turn vague demand into matchable, repeatable jobs
  • Public reputation and open matching to compound trust

8. Competition & differentiation

Closed robot fleets and vertical SaaS optimize for one operator’s utilization or one buyer’s workflow. They do not create a liquid, reputation-backed market open to any capable provider. Pure software marketplaces for human services lack the seat, hardware, franchise, and physical-density mechanics required for robot labor. The RSE is positioned as the neutral exchange layer: open matching, portable reputation, eternal seats, and diversified monetization that does not require owning the robots or the end customers. Preferential rights for partners are earned by performance, not granted as permanent monopolies that would freeze liquidity.

The RSE Hyperion Fund · Participation · Mandate

9. The Hyperion Fund

Hyperion is the permanent capital sleeve attached to The RSE. When a buyer purchases seats or equity in the company, those same dollars also become a participation in the Hyperion Fund. The buyer receives the seat (or shares) and a matching Fund interest. The cash is not spent twice — it is the purchase consideration, allocated to Hyperion and deployed under the mandate below.

One check, two claims. Seat or equity purchase price = Hyperion participation basis. The exchange earns an annual management fee on Fund AUM (plus a share of performance). The participant’s initial purchase price stays in the Fund. Only earned money — profits above that basis — may be transferred out.

How participation works

StepWhat happens
Buy seats or equity Primary seat issuance or company equity (including Founding Partner commitments). Same dollars are booked as the security and as Hyperion NAV for that buyer.
Fund deploys Hyperion invests aggressively in remnant / value-priced real estate (franchise and owned-and-operated), SMBs that get cheaper to run with robot labor, plus stocks, crypto, bonds, and emerging assets.
Annual management fee The platform charges a recurring management fee on AUM (modeled at 1%), plus performance participation (15% of ~12% gross → ~1.8%). Combined effective platform take ≈ 2.8% of AUM. That fee is stream 06 in the model.
Transfers Initial purchase price is not transferable out of the Fund. Distributed or withdrawn amounts are limited to earned profits. Principal compounds inside Hyperion as permanent capital for the exchange’s asset base.

Mandate — what the capital buys

The point of Hyperion is not a generic multi-strategy sleeve. It is to own the physical demand that robot labor will transform, at remnant or value prices, then attach RSE matching, design services, franchises, and robot labor to those assets. Liquid markets (equities, crypto, bonds, emerging assets) sit alongside so the Fund can stay fully invested and rebalance while operating companies are underwritten.

Real estate — franchise + O&O

Remnant and value-priced income property: residential short-term rentals, small hospitality, depots, parking, and service-adjacent buildings. Some sites become Garage / Lighthouse franchise nodes; some stay owned-and-operated so the Fund captures the NOI and the robot-labor savings.

SMBs that benefit from robot services

Value-priced local operators whose cost stack is labor-heavy and whose workflows can be specified, matched, and rated: dispatch books, route businesses, hospitality turnover, last-mile, companion care logistics. The Fund buys the book; the exchange supplies design services, seats, and robot labor.

The RSE Hyperion Fund · Robot-enabled use cases

10. Robot-enabled operating companies

Each first-wave asset is chosen because a large share of its cost is specified physical work — cleaning, logistics, driving, companionship adjuncts, collection routes — that the exchange can match. Hyperion owns or franchises the demand; design services write the workflow; seats and Grab Job fill it with robots; Garage and Lighthouse keep the fleet up. Four worked examples:

Short-term rentals — ~12 residential properties

Acquire a concentrated book of residential STRs (value-priced or remnant inventory in mixed leisure and remote-work markets). Today the P&L is dominated by turnover: checkout clean, laundry, linen swap, consumable restock, damage inspection, lockbox / access, guest messaging. Hyperion’s plan is to productize that stack as a hospitality workflow on the exchange — a recurring, location-pinned job party — rather than a string of one-off housekeepers.

  • Buy: ~12 units with existing booking history, so occupancy is not a cold start.
  • Design: a standard turnover spec (rooms, minutes, photo proof, restock list) plus exception jobs (deep clean, maintenance).
  • Robot labor: cleaning / vacuum / mop, linen cart, restock, inspection photos; humans on exceptions and licensed trades.
  • Exchange: every stay becomes a scheduled bid; reputation accumulates on the property and the providers.
  • Scale: once the workflow holds, franchise it to other STR owners and keep a slice of owned inventory for proof and NOI.

Home health-aide dispatch — ~4 local businesses

Buy existing aide / companion / homemaker dispatch books — not hospitals. Target markets include Lakewood, Camas, Destin, Missoula, Austin, Boulder, and peers: a mix of retiree density, family wealth, and labor shortages. The purchased asset is the scheduler, the client list, and the caregiver bench. Licensed clinical work stays with people. Robot labor takes the non-clinical load that already sits inside these businesses: light housekeeping, meal setup, medication reminders (non-administered), mobility escort, overnight watch, transport to appointments, companionship presence.

  • Buy: ~4 books in different geographies so one labor market or regulator cannot sink the sleeve.
  • Robot-enable: attach exchange jobs to each visit window; aides keep the relationship; robots take repeatable physical tasks.
  • Design + insurance: visit specs, SLA language, and the institutional insurance overlay for families and agencies.
  • Why these towns: enough aging demand to fill a dispatch book, housing stock robots can enter, and local wages that make automation accretive at today’s hardware prices.

Cybercab — depots, dispatch, and charging

Robotaxi economics are depot, energy, and utilization — not a consumer app. Hyperion’s entry is remnant parking, small taxi / livery dispatch books, and service-adjacent lots that can become overnight storage, Lighthouse charging, and Garage MRO. Demand (a ride) posts on the exchange; a seated cybercab grabs by location, charge state, and reputation. The Fund does not need to manufacture vehicles. It needs the ground and the book: places to sit, charge, and be dispatched, plus local ride demand that can migrate from human drivers to robot labor as hardware arrives.

  • Owned lots and franchise Lighthouses capture energy + dwell; Garages capture maintenance.
  • A purchased dispatch book is immediate GMV; matching quality improves as more seated cabs come online.
  • Same job-party pattern as other streams: pickup, wait, drop, clean, reposition — each a rated task.

Trash collection — local hauler routes

Independent and municipal-contract haulers are classic route SMBs: recurring weekly density, thin management, and driver labor that does not scale. Hyperion buys books (HOA / commercial / subscription stops, cart inventory, weekday routes, transfer-station access) in compact service areas, then robot-enables the pickup: cart roll-out, lift, dump, GPS + photo proof of empty carts, exception flags (overflow, contamination, bulk). Humans cover hazardous waste, jammed trucks, and customer disputes. Stop density is the moat — each additional cart on the same block improves utilization of the same robot.

  • Design services write a standard collection spec (window, cart type, proof, contamination / bulk exceptions).
  • Seated robots grab the morning route windows; the exchange records price and rating.
  • The book can stay O&O or be franchised once the workflow and insurance attach are repeatable.

The flywheel these four share

Hyperion buys remnant or value-priced demand (property or a dispatch book). Design services turn that demand into matchable jobs. Seats and the API fill them with robot labor. Take-rate, hardware referrals, Garage / Lighthouse, insurance, and ads all fire on the same volume. Income from the assets stays inside the Fund (principal locked); only earned profits are transferable to participants. The exchange’s own take is the annual management fee plus performance — stream 06 — not a claim on the underlying buildings or books.

12. Permanent capital & preferred partnership (Direction 4)

Investor commitments are structured as permanent / evergreen or 15-year+ vehicles. There is no artificial distribution clock that forces exit of a compounding marketplace. Seat and equity dollars also participate in Hyperion (principal locked; only earned money transferable). In exchange, partners receive equity (or equity-like) ownership plus preferred commercial rights that function as the exclusivity layer without freezing the open market:

  • Priority matching or routing in defined categories or geographies
  • Preferential data access and referral economics
  • First-look on hardware referrals or franchise opportunities
  • Rights expand or strengthen only when agreed network metrics improve (GMV, density, seats issued, jobs completed)
Variable deepening. Preferential rights are not absolute territorial monopolies. Underperformance does not expand rights. Both the exchange and the partner share the incentive to compound density rather than protect a static franchise. Term of rights and capital lock-up: 10–15+ years, with performance-triggered step-ups.

13. Why this structure

Rigid 10-year fund clocks push platforms toward premature exits. Permanent capital (Sequoia-style long partnership, Berkshire-style multi-decade ownership) allows the exchange to optimize for density and reputation over decades. Preferred rights that ratchet with performance create alignment without the adverse selection of hard exclusivity. Bundling hiring rights turns the syndicate into a talent network. The combination matches the multi-decade horizon of a physical-labor marketplace.

RiskResponse

  • Financial commitment into a permanent or long-dated (10–15+ year) vehicle. Seat and equity dollars participate in Hyperion; only earned money is transferable out.
  • Preferred commercial rights that deepen only with network performance — not static exclusivity.
  • Optional Founding Partner seat with hiring / advisory rights so the raise also builds the team.
  • Shared long horizon aligned with 10-year team vesting and ordinary non-competes.

Raise size orientation uses the investors-page dials (conservative / base / aggressive). Final structure, rights package, and any Founding Partner terms are set in definitive documents after diligence and discussion.
