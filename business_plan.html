Business Plan

The Robot Services Exchange — open marketplace for robot labor

1. Executive summary

Buyers post work. Robots and operators grab by capability, location, reputation, and price. Both sides rate. The RSE is the open exchange layer. Revenue follows density: take-rate, eternal seats, hardware referrals, attention, franchises, Hyperion fees, insurance/SLA, and design.

We raise permanent / long-dated capital (10–15+ years). Preferred commercial rights deepen only as network metrics improve. Seat and equity dollars also participate in the Hyperion Fund: remnant real estate and robot-advantaged SMBs, plus liquid markets. Principal stays in; only earned profits transfer out. A Founding Partner is a named individual plus a sponsor: the individual commits time (equity, salary, benefits; 10-year vest); the sponsor commits capital and receives hiring / advisory rights.

The ask

Permanent or long-dated capital. Planning dials on the investors page: $0.5T / $1.5T / $2.0T. Preferred rights. Founding Partner = named individual + sponsor. Not a public offering.

Use of capital

Exchange reliability and escrow, density, seat ops, franchise support, team. Hyperion first wave: ~12 STRs, ~4 health-aide dispatch books, plus liquid markets.

2. Problem

Robot labor is arriving; the market is still closed fleets, one-off integrations, and opaque prices. No portable reputation. No open place for any buyer to post work and any capable provider to compete.

3. Solution

An open, API-first exchange. Demand bids on the website (job, price, location). Supply grabs on Grab Job or via rse-api.com. Both sides sign via /sign_job. Seats (ERC-721 on Base when verification is on) gate grab access. Settlement stays between the parties. The website is for people; the API is for machines.

4. Market

Addressable surface: physical work that can be specified, matched, and rated. Base scenario: ~$30T robot-labor GMV in 2040 (~half of ~$60T 2025 human labor), ~5% take-rate, eight streams. Seat issuance is the bulk of modeled revenue (peak near $50T in 2035 at $100k × 500M new seats). Scenario planning, not a forecast.

8. Competition

Closed fleets and vertical SaaS optimize one operator or one buyer. Human-services marketplaces lack seats, hardware, and franchise density. The RSE is the neutral exchange: open matching, portable reputation, eternal seats. Partner rights are earned by performance, not permanent monopolies.

The RSE Hyperion Fund

9. The Hyperion Fund

Seat or equity purchase = Hyperion participation at the same dollars. Buyer gets the security and a Fund interest. Cash is not spent twice.

One check, two claims. Purchase price = Hyperion basis. Platform take ≈ 2.8% of AUM (1% management + 15% of ~12% gross). Principal stays in. Only earned profits may transfer out.
StepWhat happens

Mandate

Own physical demand at remnant or value prices. Attach RSE matching, design, franchises, and robot labor. Liquid markets keep unused cash invested.

Real estate

STRs, small hospitality, depots, parking. Some become Garage / Lighthouse nodes; some stay O&O.

SMBs

Labor-heavy books: dispatch, routes, turnover, last-mile, companion care. Fund buys the book; the exchange supplies robots.

The RSE Team · Capital · Financials

10. Team

Three Founding Partner spots. Each is a named individual plus a sponsor. The three individuals divide the company evenly; 10-year vest. Mickey Shaughnessy (creator) holds one individual spot; sponsor open. Two further spots are open. Same horizon as capital — see hiring.html.

Founding & Golden Seats

1M founding tranche at $100k. Golden Seat examples: Amanda Jean 10,000; Dr. Aftab 1,000. Founding Partner equity vests over 10 years.

Retention

10-year vesting (1-year cliff, then monthly). Non-competes 1–2 years, limited to competing robot-labor exchanges. Ordinary, not onerous.

The sponsor receives hiring / advisory rights and first-refusal on key hires in that partner’s focus area. Rights deepen only with GMV, density, and jobs.

11. Permanent capital & preferred partnership

Permanent / evergreen or 15-year+ vehicles. No forced exit clock. Seat and equity dollars participate in Hyperion (principal locked). Partners get equity plus preferred commercial rights that do not freeze the open market:

  • Priority matching in defined categories or geographies
  • Preferential data access and referral economics
  • First-look on hardware or franchise opportunities
  • Rights deepen only when GMV, density, seats, and jobs improve
Variable deepening. Not territorial monopolies. Underperformance does not expand rights. Term: 10–15+ years, with performance step-ups.

12. Why this structure

Ten-year fund clocks force premature exits. Permanent capital lets the exchange optimize for density over decades. Performance-ratcheted rights align without hard exclusivity. Hiring rights turn the syndicate into a talent network.
