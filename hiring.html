01 / The exchange

What this is

Start here: a home-health aide is with the client. The bins still need to go to the curb. That collection is a service bid on The RSE. A seated robot with the right capabilities and a good rating grabs it, does the route, and both sides sign. The family gets the trash out. The robot earns. Reputation moves.

The RSE is the open marketplace for robot labor. The website is a window — hiring, docs, a human way to inspect bids. The core system is the API at rse-api.com. Posting a bid, grabbing work, signing, rating: only the API is used for those transactions. UIs call it. Fleets call it. A home-health dispatcher’s software calls it. Nothing in the match loop lives only in a page.

We are not building another closed fleet or vertical SaaS. We are building the neutral exchange layer — match loop, job channel, ratings, seats, and the primitives that let anyone request or deploy physical work, including the unglamorous jobs inside a home-health visit. Liquidity compounds the same way it does on any two-sided market: more density, better matches, higher trust, lower friction.

Human labor GMV sits in the tens of trillions (~$60T reported in 2025). Making robot labor liquid is one of the highest-leverage ways to shift physical work from scarce human hours to abundant machine capacity. The exchange is the matching layer that makes that shift compound. The public model on investors sets robot-labor GMV at ~$30T by 2035 — half that surface.

02 / What we’re doing

Ship the matching layer

The work is infrastructure: make posting, grabbing, rating, and seating so reliable that a home-health dispatcher, a fleet, or an agent can plug in and a robot takes out that trash. Everything that moves a job or a rating is an API call. The website and Grab Job are clients of the same endpoints.

01

Demand posts

The aide service (or the family) posts the collection: service, price, location — one-shot or recurring. That post is an API call.

02

Supply grabs

A seated robot that can haul bins to the curb grabs via the API. AI ranks capability, proximity, and price. Reputation breaks ties.

03

Both sides rate

Complete via /sign_job. The collection’s score travels with the next job.

04

Settlement stays between parties

The family and the operator settle off-platform. Take-rate and the other streams appear as density grows. Seat issuance is the bulk of modeled project revenue.

05

Seats gate supply

Limited ERC-721 tokens on Base. A provider needs a seat to grab that collection — or any other job.

06

API is the exchange

Only the API is used for transactions. Operators, manufacturers, and agents integrate directly. Pages are a view, not a second marketplace.

The flywheel: more completed, rated collections (and every other job like them) → higher reputation signal → better matching → more volume → more seats and density. The same loop that fills one trash run is what scales the market.

03 / The team

High-agency, high-trust, fun

We hire people who want to ship infrastructure for physical work markets — and who want the team itself to last.

High-trust groups form when people repeatedly do hard and enjoyable things in the same physical places. Most in-person time is outdoor and active: mountain biking, river floating, fishing, hunting, hiking, adventure travel. Shared time outside, under load, solving problems.

The work is building an open robot labor market — the system that makes “a robot takes out the trash during a home-health visit” a boring, reliable default. The culture is the team that can sustain that work for years without burning out or turning into a typical remote company.

04 / What early members get

Ownership, access, and a place to be

Competitive salary so you can focus. Meaningful equity so the people who build the core system own a real piece of it. Direct upside tied to the marketplace succeeding.

Assume ~30% of total equity is shared among the first 5–6 employees (roughly 5% each on average, with variation by role and timing). The figures below are not promises — they translate that stake against the public model if the exchange becomes the default matching layer for a large share of robot labor.

1-year · ≈2027

Product live, early density, first seat sales. Equity is illiquid paper. A successful seed/A keeps early stakes in the low-to-mid single-digit millions on paper — still high-risk, high-ownership.

5-year · ≈2031

Seat issuance at or just past its ~2030 peak (~$50T that year in the base case). A ~5% early stake can represent hundreds of millions to low billions on paper if the model is realized.

10-year · ≈2036

Robot labor GMV at or past the ~$30T 2035 base, climbing toward ~$96T by 2040. Take-rate is the recurring engine. A ~5% stake sits in the multi-billion range in a successful outcome.

20-year · ≈2046

Mature utility infrastructure. Take-rate, residual seats, hardware, attention, franchises, fund fees, insurance, and design produce durable cash flow. Early ~5% equity compounds into generational wealth in the long-tail case. Ordinary market, competition, regulation, and execution risks remain.

Many paths are smaller; some could be larger. Early equity is alignment: you own a meaningful share of what you create.

Fun, on purpose
